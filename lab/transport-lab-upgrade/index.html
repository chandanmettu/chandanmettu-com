What changed, and why it matters

1. A stability bug in the explicit step

The lab takes Δt = min(0.42 ρcpΔx²/k, 0.7 Δx/u). Explicit upwind advection–diffusion needs the two together, C + 2r ≤ 1 (and less at the half-cell boundary faces). With the default conductivity and the flow slider at 30 %, C + 2r ≈ 1.09, and the field reaches 10¹⁸⁰ K while the balance panel still reads BALANCED: the scheme is conservative, so the ledger closes even as the solution explodes.

2. First-order upwind smears the field

Upwind face values add numerical diffusion of about uΔx/2. On the lab's 64 cells that is a 20 K error at Pe ≈ 38 that converges only at first order. The exponential scheme (Patankar 1980) uses the exact 1D advection–diffusion profile between neighbouring cells, so for constant properties without a source the steady cell values are exact at any Péclet number, on any grid.

flux across a face = F·Tup − A·(Tdown − Tup), F = ρcpu, A = F/(eF·d/k − 1)

3. Crank–Nicolson in time

One tridiagonal solve per step, using the lab's own solver, second order in time and stable at any step (two backward-Euler steps first, after Rannacher, to damp the jump at the left face). Try “Upgraded step 16×”.

Where it does not win

For pure conduction the lab's explicit scheme is already accurate (about 0.03 K at 10 s); at the same step the upgrade matches it within a few hundredths of a kelvin. The gain is in advection, stability and step size, not in conduction.

Measured on the lab's 64 cells

  • Steady, flow 10 / 30 / 100 %: his 8.3 / 19.9 / 33.7 K, upgraded 2×10⁻¹¹ / 7×10⁻¹² / 5×10⁻¹³ K.
  • Transient, flow 10 %, t = 3 s, against a 2048-cell reference: his 5.6 K, upgraded 0.34 K.
  • Transient, flow 30 %: his diverges; upgraded within 1.5 K.
