The instrument

Three systems working as one microscope

Optics

A compound lens train magnifies the sample and a webcam turns the view into a live digital image.

10× objective · 15× eyepiece · 160 mm tube

Motion

Two joysticks command stepper motors that focus on Z and move the field of view on X.

Arduino Uno · 2 × 28BYJ-48 · ULN2003

Intelligence

Live webcam frames go to an image model that names the sample on screen with a confidence score.

    Imaging modes

    Two ways to light a sample

    Reflected light

    For opaque surfaces

    Light bounces off the metal back into the objective. Scratches and machining marks change the reflection angle, so they show up as bright and dark streaks.

    Transmitted light

    For thin, see-through samples

    Light passes up through the specimen. Differences in thickness and absorption create the contrast between cells, walls and veins.

    Results · fine features

    Screen subpixels show what the optics can resolve

    RGB subpixel array
    Patterned illuminated display
    Why a screen

    A display is a ready-made test target: a regular grid of red, green and blue emitters at microscale.

    Seeing each coloured subpixel as a separate dot shows the microscope resolves repeating microscale structure.

    The pixel pitch was not calibrated, so this is a qualitative check.

    Outcome

    What the prototype shows, and where it goes next

    Demonstrated

    • Motorized X and Z positioning from two joysticks.
    • Live digital imaging through a webcam on a 160 mm tube.
    • Two light modes: reflected for metals, transmitted for thin samples.
    • Live classification of empty field, rough metal and scratches.

    Not yet measured

    • Calibrated magnification of the camera image.
    • Resolution in micrometres, e.g. display pixel pitch.
    • Classifier accuracy on a held-out test set.
    • Grain and phase features, which need etched samples.

    Proposed next steps

    • Stage micrometer calibration for a true scale bar.
    • Autofocus by sweeping Z and maximising image sharpness.
    • More classes such as leaf and onion, with a test split.
    • Etched metal samples to study microstructure.
